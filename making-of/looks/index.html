The art direction notes for the anniversary edition open with one line: a paper-cut world, seen from a real seat, at speed. The world is folded from colored paper: flat faces, one color each, hard creases, no texture on anything. The cockpit is built to real scale, and stereo makes it read as real, so the cockpit feels solid and the islands look like paper.

Classic is a look

The game ships two looks. Classic is 2016 as faithfully as the port can draw it, kept bugs and all. Anniversary is a set of deliberate departures, each one written down with its reason and each one a switch in a preset file. Both looks fly the same race by the same rules. Switching only changes what you see, so you can compare them with one press.

A ramp for the light

One of those departures is how a facet answers the sun. Ordinary diffuse lighting gives a face turned away from the light nothing at all, so on a faceted island every face on the shadow side of a ridge is the same flat dark. Anniversary borrows a trick from Team Fortress 2 and looks the light up on a ramp instead: cool in the shadow, warm right at the edge where the light grazes, and bright on top. On smooth shapes that's a soft band. On this terrain, where every face is one flat color over tens of square meters, it's a per-face color, and it lets you tell two neighboring facets apart by more than brightness.

Try it: move the sun slider until most of the dome faces away. Click Classic (off) and the back of the dome goes black. Click Anniversary and it takes a cool fill, with a warm band where the faces are edge-on to the sun. The dashed line is the plain response, and the three colored ones are the ramp, per channel. 



      
Too clean, or sludge

      

        Every change to the look gets judged against two ways to fail. Too clean, and the world
        turns into a pastel toy with no sense of size. Too far the other way, and it turns to
        sludge: fog, bloom and soft creases until you can't count the faces on a cliff. The fog
        has already been in that second ditch once. I thinned it by eye, and it's held below a
        measured ceiling now.
