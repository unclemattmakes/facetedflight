In 2016 you raced recordings. They were ghosts: the same line every time, flown once and replayed. The anniversary edition races five autopilots that fly the course for real, with the same physics you have, and each one is a little different, and none of them is meant to be clever.

What a ghost was

A 2016 ghost is a recording and nothing else: a long list of poses, one every so often, and the game draws the ship wherever the clock says it was, blending between the two nearest. There's no physics in it and it can't react. It flies the same line at the same speed every time, so the port races autopilots instead and keeps the recordings only as a fallback.

Try it: press play, then drag clock back and forth. Tick show the recorded keys: every dot is one recorded pose, and everything between them is the game's interpolation.

A line through the gates

An autopilot needs somewhere to go. The racers follow a line drawn through the course's gates, a polyline of a few hundred points, and they steer toward a spot a little way ahead along it. How far ahead matters a lot: too far and they cut corners, too near and they wobble.

Tuning that distance went wrong. A figure was measured, "the gates sit up to 24 meters off the racing line", and a constant was built on it. The figure was the distance from each gate to the nearest corner of the line, and the corners are about 27 meters apart. A gate can sit in the middle of a long straight segment and be a long way from either end while being almost exactly on the line. The distance to the line itself was about a meter, and the constant cost seven seconds a lap.

Try it: drag gate to step through the gates. The blue circle, the distance to the line, is small and steady. The red one, the distance to the nearest corner, swings about. Raise thin the line (it reads as shipped at 1) and the corners spread out, so the red number balloons while the blue one moves far less. A line with fewer points cuts the bends, so it does move. zoom to the gate gets you closer.
